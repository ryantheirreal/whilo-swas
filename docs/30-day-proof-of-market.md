# Whilo — 30-day proof-of-market sprint

Updated: 19 October 2026

## A measurable objective, not a valuation promise

Sequoia's March 5, 2026 essay, [Services: The New Software](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software), argues that the next giant AI business may look like a services company while its software delivers the work. Its key distinction is copilot (selling tools) versus autopilot (selling the completed job), with outsourced, intelligence-heavy work as the entry wedge. This is a strategy thesis, not a forecast of Whilo's valuation.

A US$1 billion valuation within 30 days cannot be responsibly promised from the current starting point. It depends on independent investors, credible market size, execution, growth, retention, margins and diligence. The controllable 30-day goal is to build evidence that could justify investor conversations: paid outcomes, repeatable delivery, customer renewal intent, measured cost/quality and a credible path to expand.

## The entry offer: Revenue Operator for B2B service businesses

Start with one repeated workflow that companies already pay an agency, SDR provider or contractor to perform. This is not one of the ten verticals explicitly listed in Sequoia's illustrative map; it is an application of the same outsourced-budget principle, selected as a lower-regulation way to test Whilo's existing B2B revenue direction.

**Sell a completed weekly outcome, not access to a bot:**

- Documented target-account research against an agreed ideal customer profile.
- Personalized outreach/campaign drafts and a follow-up queue.
- CRM hygiene and next-step preparation using approved sources.
- A weekly evidence pack: work completed, exceptions, human corrections, pipeline movement where verified and recommendations for the next week.

External messages are drafts until a human approves sending. Do not promise a specific number of leads, meetings or revenue before the baseline, attribution method and quality criteria are agreed. Pricing should be tested through paid pilots rather than announced as market-validated.

## Days 1–3: define the product and acceptance contract

1. Select one narrow customer segment (for example, B2B agencies or software/services firms with an existing outbound or RevOps provider). Pick one buyer and one repeatable job.
2. Interview 15–20 qualified buyers about their current vendor, spend, delays, quality problems and what would make them switch. Record exact buyer language and the decision process.
3. Define the job contract: inputs, allowed sources, deliverables, acceptance checklist, turnaround time, exceptions, what needs approval and what is outside scope.
4. Publish a one-page offer and a short workflow demo. The demo must label simulated/local tracking separately from real system actions.
5. Build a human-operated pilot SOP. Until the remote runtime and integrations are real, the service can be delivered with the founder/human review plus model-assisted drafting; do not pretend the browser queue autonomously executes.

**Exit criteria:** one clear offer, one buyer, one delivery runbook, baseline metrics template and a signed-off definition of done.

## Days 4–10: sell pilots before widening the product

Run personalized, permission-respecting outreach to qualified businesses with evidence of existing spend in the chosen workflow. Aim for 30–50 real buyer conversations over the first ten days, not mass spam. Ask for the problem and existing budget before pitching features.

Test a setup fee plus a monthly service fee, using a limited paid pilot with clear scope, a start/end date and an acceptance clause. A US$1k–5k pilot range can be tested against the buyer segment, but it is only a pricing hypothesis; the customer must validate willingness to pay.

**Targets, not guarantees:** 5–10 qualified proposals, 2–3 paid pilots signed, and a documented reason for every loss. Avoid free pilots with no buyer access or success criteria.

## Days 11–20: deliver and measure outcomes

Each pilot uses the same checklist and a human remains accountable for quality and sensitive decisions. Record for each job: input provenance, output delivered, first-pass acceptance, corrections, time spent, compute/tool spend, cycle time and exceptions. Keep client data isolated and do not reuse it for another customer without permission.

Suggested operating targets for the experiment (not promises to buyers):

- At least 80% of deliverables accepted on first review after the initial calibration period.
- Human rework under 20% of delivery time and trending down.
- No external sends or account changes without an explicit approval record.
- Positive contribution margin after counting human review, model/tool cost and customer-specific setup.
- Two or more pilot customers willing to pay for the next month, based on accepted work rather than demo excitement.

If the workflow misses these marks, narrow scope or improve the process; don't mask low quality by adding more Bots.

## Days 21–30: turn proof into repeatable distribution

1. Publish one customer-approved case study with baseline, scope, time, acceptance rate and limits. Never invent attributed revenue.
2. Convert successful pilots to recurring service contracts with a weekly delivery cadence and clear escalation owner.
3. Package the repeated SOP as a versioned playbook. Only automate the parts with stable inputs, measurable acceptance and recoverable failures.
4. Use the results in investor conversations: paid customer references, expansion potential, repeatable acquisition channel, contribution margin, quality/rework curve, and customer renewal intent.
5. Decide the next function only after the first one renews. Sequoia's thesis supports expanding from outsourced intelligence toward more judgment-heavy work as domain data compounds; it does not support launching ten departments with unproven delivery.

## What Whilo must build alongside the service

**Agentic execution:** durable mission states, Pet accountability, task-scoped Bots, dependency graph, constrained tools, approval gates, independent evidence verification, timeout recovery and an audit receipt.

**Enterprise readiness:** server-side identity/tenancy, durable database, cross-device sessions, least-privilege connector grants, token revocation, per-customer secrets, retention policy and observable cost per accepted output.

**Real Computer Runtime:** a remote isolated Linux session, live screenshot and control bridge, lifecycle management, secure egress, persistent session storage policy and recovery verification. The current browser-local /api emulator does not satisfy this requirement.

**Desktop:** first get reproducible macOS/Windows/Linux packages with safe defaults and a shared agent core. Local keyboard/screen control is a separate, consent-gated feature—not something enabled by simply wrapping the website.

## Valuation discipline

A trillion-dollar outcome would require a massive durable business, not a crowded navigation bar. In the next 30 days, optimize for evidence investors can diligence: customers paying for a clearly bounded outcome, renewals, repeatable customer acquisition, improving quality, measurable delivery economics, a large adjacent market and a credible defensibility story. Treat a US$1B valuation as an ambition; don't use it as a delivery promise or as a reason to misstate the current system's capabilities.
